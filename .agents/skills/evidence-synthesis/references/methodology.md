# Evidence Synthesis Methodology Reference

## Synthesis Types

### Narrative Synthesis
Best for: Heterogeneous studies, qualitative/mixed methods, exploratory reviews.

Approach:
1. Describe patterns across studies using words rather than statistics
2. Organize findings thematically
3. Describe relationships between study characteristics and findings
4. Assess robustness based on methodological quality

### Scoping Synthesis
Best for: Mapping a research area, identifying gaps, clarifying concepts.

Approach:
1. Broad inclusion criteria
2. Chart key characteristics of included studies
3. Analyze themes and gaps
4. Do NOT assess quality (distinguishes from systematic review)

### Meta-Analytic Synthesis
Best for: Homogeneous quantitative studies with comparable effect sizes.

Approach:
1. Extract effect sizes from each study
2. Compute pooled effect size (fixed or random effects model)
3. Assess heterogeneity (I² statistic)
4. Test for publication bias (funnel plot, Egger's test)
5. Perform subgroup and sensitivity analyses

### Framework Synthesis
Best for: Policy-relevant questions with an a priori framework.

Approach:
1. Define conceptual framework before synthesis
2. Map findings to framework categories
3. Identify where framework is supported/challenged
4. Revise framework based on evidence

## Consensus Assessment Scale

### Level 1: Unanimous Consensus
- All studies agree on direction and magnitude
- Confidence: **Very High**
- Report: "There is strong, consistent evidence that..."

### Level 2: Strong Consensus
- >80% of studies agree, remaining are neutral
- Confidence: **High**
- Report: "The evidence largely supports that..."

### Level 3: Moderate Consensus
- 60-80% of studies agree
- Confidence: **Moderate**
- Report: "The majority of evidence suggests..., though some studies find..."

### Level 4: Mixed Evidence
- 40-60% agreement, significant disagreement
- Confidence: **Low**
- Report: "Evidence is inconsistent, with approximately equal support for and against..."

### Level 5: Strong Disagreement
- <40% agreement
- Confidence: **Very Low**
- Report: "There is substantial disagreement in the literature regarding..."

## Quality Assessment Tools

### Randomized Controlled Trials
- Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool (RoB 2)
- Assess: randomization, deviations from intended interventions, missing data, measurement, selection of reported result

### Observational Studies
- Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS)
- Assess: selection, comparability, outcome

### Systematic Reviews
- AMSTAR 2
- Assess: search strategy, duplicate screening, data extraction, risk of bias assessment

### Qualitative Studies
- Critical Appraisal Skills Programme (CASP)
- Assess: research design, recruitment, data collection, analysis, findings

## Handling Common Synthesis Challenges

### High Heterogeneity
When studies differ substantially:
1. Check if all studies address the same question
2. Perform subgroup analysis by study characteristics
3. Consider narrative synthesis instead of meta-analysis
4. Report I² and explore sources of heterogeneity

### Small Number of Studies
When <5 studies are available:
1. Avoid meta-analysis (insufficient power)
2. Use narrative synthesis
3. Note the limited evidence base
4. Recommend further research

### Publication Bias
When positive results are overrepresented:
1. Check for unpublished studies (clinical trial registries, conference abstracts)
2. Perform funnel plot analysis (if >10 studies)
3. Apply trim-and-fill method
4. Acknowledge potential bias in conclusions

### Conflicting High-Quality Studies
When well-designed studies disagree:
1. Examine population differences
2. Compare intervention details
3. Consider contextual factors (setting, timing)
4. Present both sides without forcing consensus
5. Identify specific research needed to resolve the conflict
