# Citation Formats Reference

## APA 7th Edition

### Journal Article
```
Author, A. A., Author, B. B., & Author, C. C. (Year). Title of article. Title of
    Periodical, volume(issue), page–page. https://doi.org/xxxxx
```

Example:
```
Smith, J. D., Jones, K. L., & Lee, M. R. (2024). Novel approaches to CRISPR gene
    editing in mammalian cells. Nature Medicine, 30(4), 123–135.
    https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-01234-5
```

### Book
```
Author, A. A. (Year). Title of work: Capital letter also for subtitle. Publisher.
```

### Chapter in Edited Book
```
Author, A. A. (Year). Title of chapter. In E. E. Editor (Ed.), Title of book
    (pp. xx–xx). Publisher.
```

### Conference Paper
```
Author, A. A. (Year). Title of paper. In Proceedings of the Conference Name
    (pp. xx–xx). Publisher. https://doi.org/xxxxx
```

### Online Source
```
Author, A. A. (Year, Month Day). Title of page. Website Name. URL
```

## Vancouver Style

### Journal Article
```
Author AA, Author BB, Author CC. Title of article. Title of Periodical. Year;Volume(Issue):Page-Page.
```

Example:
```
Smith JD, Jones KL, Lee MR. Novel approaches to CRISPR gene editing in mammalian cells. Nat Med. 2024;30(4):123-135.
```

### Book
```
Author AA. Title of book. Edition. Place: Publisher; Year.
```

## BibTeX

### Article
```bibtex
@article{smith2024novel,
  author  = {Smith, John D. and Jones, Kate L. and Lee, Michael R.},
  title   = {Novel Approaches to {CRISPR} Gene Editing in Mammalian Cells},
  journal = {Nature Medicine},
  volume  = {30},
  number  = {4},
  pages   = {123--135},
  year    = {2024},
  doi     = {10.1038/s41591-024-01234-5}
}
```

### Book
```bibtex
@book{author2024title,
  author    = {Author, First},
  title     = {Title of Book},
  publisher = {Publisher Name},
  year      = {2024},
  address   = {City}
}
```

### Conference
```bibtex
@inproceedings{author2024title,
  author    = {Author, First},
  title     = {Title of Paper},
  booktitle = {Proceedings of Conference Name},
  pages     = {123--135},
  year      = {2024},
  doi       = {10.xxxx/yyyy}
}
```

## MLA 9th Edition

### Journal Article
```
Author Last, First, et al. "Title of Article." Title of Journal, vol. X, no. Y,
    Year, pp. XX-YY.
```

## Chicago 17th Edition

### Notes-Bibliography (Humanities)
```
Author Last, First. "Title of Article." Title of Journal X, no. Y (Year): XX-YY.
```

### Author-Date (Sciences)
```
Author Last, First. Year. "Title of Article." Title of Journal X (Y): XX-YY.
```

## Citation with Annotation Format

### APA Annotated
```
Smith, J. D., Jones, K. L., & Lee, M. R. (2024). Novel approaches to CRISPR gene
    editing in mammalian cells. Nature Medicine, 30(4), 123–135.
    https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-01234-5

    This study investigated CRISPR editing efficiency in human cell lines. The authors
    conducted a series of in vitro experiments (n=50 cell lines) comparing their novel
    delivery method with standard viral transduction. Key findings include a 3-fold
    improvement in editing efficiency (p<0.001) and reduced off-target effects. This
    paper is highly relevant to our work on gene therapy delivery as it provides the
    strongest evidence to date for the proposed method's superiority. Quality: High
    (well-controlled experiments, appropriate statistics, pre-registered).
```

### BibTeX Annotated
```bibtex
@article{smith2024novel,
  author    = {Smith, John D. and Jones, Kate L. and Lee, Michael R.},
  title     = {Novel Approaches to {CRISPR} Gene Editing in Mammalian Cells},
  journal   = {Nature Medicine},
  volume    = {30},
  number    = {4},
  pages     = {123--135},
  year      = {2024},
  doi       = {10.1038/s41591-024-01234-5},
  annotation = {Investigated CRISPR editing in 50 human cell lines. Found 3x
    improvement over standard method (p<0.001). Highly relevant: strongest evidence
    for novel delivery method. Quality: High. Relevance: Essential.},
  relevance = {essential},
  quality   = {high}
}
```

## Hanging Indent Convention

For all prose-format bibliographies:
- First line: flush left
- Subsequent lines: 0.5 inch (1.27 cm) indent
- This applies to APA, MLA, and Chicago styles

```
Smith, J. D., Jones, K. L., & Lee, M. R. (2024). Novel approaches to CRISPR
    gene editing in mammalian cells. Nature Medicine, 30(4), 123–135.
    https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-01234-5

    Annotation text starts here with a standard paragraph indent...
```
