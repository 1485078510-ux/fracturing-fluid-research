Having validated the decomposition approach on single-phase data, we examined whether the same model can handle the more realistic condition of two-phase flow. In the field, the single-phase condition corresponds to the early production period following flowback, when the well produces primarily oil with minimal water cut. The two-phase condition represents the later stage of the well's life, when formation water breaks through and co-produces with oil; under these conditions, distinguishing the oil contribution of each stage from the total liquid output becomes considerably more challenging, yet operationally more valuable. Steady-state core displacement experiments spanned three oil-water ratios (OWR = 4:1, 1:1, 1:4) and four total flow rates (0.1-0.4 mL/min). Direct observation of the effluent at OWR = 1:1 revealed a higher oil fraction in the early-stage effluent, consistent with the oil-permeable character of ESP-T established in Section 3.5.
