The interpretation model is derived from the one-dimensional advection-dispersion equation (ADE): partialC/partialt + v times partialC/partialx = D times partial2C/partialx2, where v = 4Q/(pi d2) is the mean flow velocity (cm/min) and D = alpha v is the longitudinal dispersion coefficient (cm2/min), with Q the effective oil-phase volumetric flow rate (cm3/min), d the tubing inner diameter (cm), and alpha the longitudinal dispersivity (cm). For a semi-infinite domain with an instantaneous slug of tracer mass M injected at x = 0, t = 0, the classical ADE analytical solution yields a Gaussian pulse: C_rise(x,t) = (M/A)/sqrt(4pi D t) exp[-(x - v t)2 / (4 D t)], which describes the advective-dispersive transport of the tracer accumulated in the proppant pack during the well shut-in period. For a continuous source maintaining a constant concentration C0 at the inlet, the ADE solution takes the complementary-error-function form: C_fall(x,t) = (C0/2) erfc[(x - v t) / sqrt(4 D t)], which captures the sustained release of residual nano-Fe3O4@SA from the epoxy matrix after the main concentration pulse has passed. These two transport regimes—the shut-in slug and the matrix-diffusion-controlled tail—operate simultaneously during production. To construct a physically continuous composite model that avoids the non-differentiable discontinuity of a piecewise step transition, we blend the two analytical solutions via a hyperbolic-tangent weighting function w(t) = 1/2 [1 + tanh((t - t0)/sigma)], where t0 is the crossover center and sigma controls the transition width.
