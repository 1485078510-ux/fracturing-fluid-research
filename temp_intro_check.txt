=== [7] ===
Sustained growth in global energy demand has positioned unconventional oil and gas resources at the center of contemporary petroleum development [1-4]. These resources now account for more than 50% of the world's total hydrocarbon reserves [5], and their efficient development is critical to global energy security. Hydraulic fracturing, the core stimulation technology for low-permeability and tight formations, creates artificial fractures that substantially enhance hydrocarbon seepage pathways and well productivity [6].

=== [9] ===
However, a persistent operational challenge follows stimulation: the oil production contribution of each fractured interval varies markedly, and interval productivity declines at different rates as field development proceeds [7]. Without reliable per-interval production data, reservoir productivity assessment and subsequent development strategy optimization lack a quantitative foundation, ultimately compromising the overall hydrocarbon recovery factor. Accurate, interval-level production monitoring is therefore not merely desirable but essential for the economical development of unconventional resources.

=== [13] ===
Compounding this methodological gap, the materials employed in existing tracer proppants face their own practical limitations. Coated proppants suffer from high particle density that impedes transport in fracturing fluids, and monitoring ceases abruptly once the polymer coating dissolves. Pure PS microspheres, while enabling oil-phase tracer incorporation, exhibit inadequate thermal stability and mechanical strength under downhole conditions [27-30]. Epoxy resin, a thermosetting polymer whose highly cross-linked network imparts outstanding thermal stability (decomposition temperature exceeding 350 °C), exceptional chemical resistance, and tunable mechanical properties, offers a compelling alternative matrix material [31,32]. Direct emulsion-polymerization synthesis of epoxy resin microspheres further avoids the coating-uniformity and interfacial-debonding risks inherent in multi-step coating processes, enabling low-density tailoring, nanoparticle modification, and in-situ tracer encapsulation within a single synthetic step. Li et al. [33] and Wei et al. [34] demonstrated that epoxy matrices can serve as carriers for water-soluble tracers in aqueous-phase inflow profiling. Extending this concept to the oil phase requires rendering the epoxy matrix oleophilic, so that it releases its tracer payload selectively into the oil rather than the aqueous phase, and, critically, integrating the material with a predictive transport model that translates the resulting tracer signals into quantitative per-interval production allocation. Neither the oleophilic epoxy tracer matrix nor its coupling with such a model has been reported.

=== [15] ===
Here we address this dual gap. We develop a piecewise advection-dispersion (ADE) model in which the tracer breakthrough curve is decomposed into two physically distinct components linked by a smooth hyperbolic-tangent (tanh) transition: a Gaussian pulse representing the advective-dispersive transport of the tracer slug accumulated during the well shut-in period, and an erfc tail representing the sustained, matrix-diffusion-controlled release of residual tracer from the polymer carrier. This decomposition establishes a direct quantitative relationship between the measured tracer concentration history and the per-interval oil production rate, transforming parameters that would otherwise remain as empirical fitting coefficients into physically interpretable quantities. To validate this model, we designed and synthesized an oleophilic tracer proppant (ESP-T) in which stearic acid-modified nano-Fe3O4 (nano-Fe3O4@SA) is encapsulated within an epoxy resin matrix via emulsion polymerization. We systematically characterize ESP-T's microstructure, thermal stability, wettability, oil-water transport selectivity, and mechanical integrity; quantify its temperature-dependent release kinetics via the Korsmeyer-Peppas model; and validate the integrated material-model framework through single-phase and steady-state two-phase core displacement experiments. Together, this work establishes a coupled experimental-modeling methodology that translates sustained tracer release signals into quantitative per-interval production allocation, a broadly applicable framework for unconventional reservoir management.
